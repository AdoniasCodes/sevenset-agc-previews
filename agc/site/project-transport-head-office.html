Four months. Zero excuses.

The Ministry of Transport building at Megenagna, Addis Ababa. A 1.5 billion birr envelope package that many in the market said would take years.

The brief

A head office, and a deadline that was already public.

The Transport Sectoral Head Office is a government building for the Addis Ababa City Administration. The structure was up. What remained was the part everybody sees and the part that is hardest to programme: the envelope and the interior.

The scope covered curtain walls, aluminum paneling and sunbreaker installation and finishing works across the building. Each of those is a separate trade with its own lead times, its own shop drawings and its own tolerance for error, which is close to none where a facade meets structure.

The programme was four months. That is not a normal programme for an envelope package of this size, and it was not treated as one.

Scope of works

What AGC was responsible for.

  1. 01

    Curtain wall

    Design coordination, fabrication and installation of the curtain wall system, sealed and tested for weather and water performance.

  2. 02

    Aluminum paneling

    Aluminum cladding panels across the elevations, set out to line and level against a structure that was already built.

  3. 03

    Sunbreaker installation

    External sunbreakers fixed to the facade, adding a second layer of setting out tolerance to work around.

  4. 04

    Finishing works

    Interior completion, surface finishes and detailing, running in parallel with the envelope rather than waiting for it.

How it was done

Parallel, not sequential.

A four month envelope package cannot be run in sequence. Survey, shop drawings, fabrication, mobilization and installation were overlapped, with the survey feeding fabrication in batches rather than all at once.

Owned machinery mattered here. Plant that belongs to the contractor does not wait behind another client's programme, and fabrication done in house does not queue behind another supplier's order book.

Quality verification ran alongside installation rather than at the end of it, so that a defect found on the third elevation did not become a defect repeated on all four. That sequence is described in full under project management.

The building was handed over in four months. It is the project we point at when a client asks whether a date is realistic.
